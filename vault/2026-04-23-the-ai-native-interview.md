# The AI-Native Interview

## Summary
Sierra argues coding agents have changed software engineering enough that interviews should measure outcome judgment, not syntax mechanics. Their new process replaces coding drills with a Plan-Build-Review onsite that mirrors real work and surfaces stronger hiring signal.

The format evaluates whether candidates can define scope, make product and technical tradeoffs, use AI tools effectively, and move from idea to demo under time pressure. Sierra also shifted its phone screen to system design and is piloting a debugging interview for 1->N feature work in messy codebases, aiming to hire for strengths and role fit rather than generic pass/fail performance.

## Key Ideas
- Coding agents compress implementation effort, so leverage shifts toward judgment, initiative, and product context.
- Traditional interviews overweight mechanical coding signal and under-measure day-to-day impact skills.
- A representative onsite should test ideation, execution with AI tools, and reasoning about production readiness.
- Hiring debriefs become more useful when framed as "where will this person thrive?" instead of binary hire/no-hire.
- Infrastructure hiring can still use this format with slight signal adjustments.

## Insights & Claims
- The strongest modern engineers combine technical execution with product and business thinking.
- Plan-Build-Review yields clearer signal on agency (pivoting when blocked) and scope judgment.
- Replacing AI-free coding screens with system design better reflects what is difficult in practice.
- Open-ended interviews can be calibrated with role-agnostic criteria and paired interviewers.

## Actionable Takeaways
- Redesign interviews around real workflows candidates will perform after joining.
- Evaluate AI-tool usage explicitly, including where candidates delegate vs. reason manually.
- Separate 0->1 product building signal from 1->N codebase evolution signal.
- Share criteria ahead of time so candidates optimize for decision quality, not performative completeness.

## Notable Quotes
> "If it's great, it doesn't have to be good."

## Why This Matters
- Interview loops that lag behind tooling shifts miss high-upside candidates and overvalue obsolete signals.
- AI-native evaluation improves both hiring quality and candidate experience.

## Applications
- Rebuild interview loops for agent-era product engineering teams.
- Create rubric-based build sessions for startup hiring where ownership and speed matter.
- Add debugging/PR-improvement rounds to test scaling judgment in existing systems.
