# Anthropic's Boris Cherny: Why Coding Is Solved, and What Comes Next

## Summary
At Sequoia's AI Ascent 2026, Boris Cherny (creator of Claude Code at Anthropic) argues that day-to-day coding is rapidly shifting from manual typing to agent orchestration and tight feedback loops.

He describes a workflow where he ships many PRs from a phone, treats loops as the core abstraction for software work, and expects tool builders to automate increasingly larger chunks of implementation. The talk frames this as a structural shift in software creation rather than just a productivity upgrade.

## Key Ideas
- "Coding is solved" for many practical tasks when strong agent tooling is in the loop.
- The new bottleneck is increasingly judgment, product taste, and loop design, not syntax output speed.
- High-leverage workflows are moving from IDE-centric typing to orchestration across devices and contexts.
- Teams may trend toward smaller groups of generalists coordinating agents.

## Insights & Claims
- Boris claims he personally writes little to no direct code in 2026 for his own workflow.
- Claude Code-like systems may become dramatically simpler over time as abstractions mature.
- AI loops (prompt, evaluate, refine, merge) are becoming the dominant development pattern.

## Actionable Takeaways
- Build your workflow around repeatable agent loops (draft -> review -> patch -> test) instead of one-shot prompts.
- Optimize for review velocity and PR quality, since those become central in an agent-first setup.
- Practice "mobile command" habits (issue triage, review, merge decisions) to reduce dependence on one workspace.
- Invest in evaluation scaffolding and guardrails if you want higher autonomous throughput.

## Why This Matters
- It signals a shift from "how fast can I code?" to "how well can I orchestrate and validate?".
- This changes hiring, team shape, and the daily craft of software engineering.

## Applications
- Designing internal engineering workflows for AI-first product teams.
- Defining new engineering career ladders around review quality, systems thinking, and orchestration.
- Building developer tools that focus on loop reliability and governance over autocomplete.
